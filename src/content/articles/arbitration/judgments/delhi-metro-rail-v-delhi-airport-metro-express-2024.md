## Procedural setting
The Supreme Court considered curative petitions after its earlier appellate judgment restored an arbitral award in a metro concession dispute.

## Reasoning and disposition
The Court concluded that the High Court Division Bench had applied the proper Section 34 standard when examining the award. The earlier Supreme Court intervention had produced a grave miscarriage of justice. The curative petitions were allowed and the Division Bench's position restored (paragraphs 68–73).

## Ratio and limits
The reasoning links patent illegality with disregard of decisive record material rather than an ordinary disagreement about evidentiary weight. The Court expressly warned against using curative jurisdiction as another routine tier of arbitral review (paragraph 70).

## Practice implications
Distinguish the statutory grounds under Sections 34 and 37 from the exceptional constitutional curative procedure. Identify the precise omitted evidence, the relevant award reasoning and its legal significance before characterising a finding as perverse.
