## Background
A highway construction contract contained a price adjustment mechanism. The arbitral majority applied a different formula based on a unilateral circular; the contractor challenged the award.

## Reasoning and scope
Paragraphs 26–30 distinguish the restricted public-policy inquiry from the additional patent-illegality ground in Section 34(2A). Courts cannot conduct a fresh evidentiary assessment. The Court discusses absence of reasons, impossible contractual interpretations and disregard of crucial evidence as examples within the domestic-award framework. Section 34(2A) did not apply to the international commercial arbitration before it.

## Disposition and practical relevance
In paragraphs 48–50, the Court treated the imposition of a new bargain as an exceptional violation of basic justice. It annulled the majority award and invoked Article 142 to give effect to the minority award. That constitutional relief is not a general power of a Section 34 court.
