## What happened

The Supreme Court restored a contractor's recovery suit that the Delhi High Court had rejected as time-barred. The original plaintiff, Wazir Hussain, brought an Order XXXVII CPC suit in 2014 concerning construction bills from 2000–2003. The plaintiff relied on entries carried forward in the defendant society's accounts until 2013, together with reconciliation records linking the contractor to dishonoured cheques.

## Legal significance

The judgment applies Section 18 of the Limitation Act, 1963. A qualifying written acknowledgment signed before expiry can start a fresh limitation period. Accounting entries must be examined with their surrounding documents and any qualifications. The Court considered the materials sufficient to prevent rejection at the threshold under Order VII Rule 11 CPC.

## What remains undecided

The Court did not award the amount claimed or finally determine liability. It restored the suit for trial and left the merits open. Practitioners should preserve successive signed financial statements and documents connecting the balance-sheet entry to the particular debt; a later acknowledgment after limitation has expired does not automatically revive a claim under Section 18.
