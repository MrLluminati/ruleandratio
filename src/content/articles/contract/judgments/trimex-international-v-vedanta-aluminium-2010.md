## Background
A proposed bauxite supply was negotiated electronically. The parties disputed whether their correspondence bound them before a formal instrument was executed.

## Reasoning and outcome
The Court found assent to the essential commercial terms after a conditional response was replaced by unqualified acceptance. The contemplated paperwork was not a precondition in that bargain. In the arbitration appointment proceeding, the Court accepted the existence of an arbitration agreement in the exchanged materials.

## Practical limits
Electronic correspondence is not automatically binding. Identify the authority of senders, final terms, reservations and statutory formalities; distinguish the historical Section 11 appointment framework from subsequent changes.
