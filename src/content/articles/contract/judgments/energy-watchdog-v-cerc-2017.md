## Background
Power generators sought compensatory tariffs following changes affecting fuel procurement costs under long-term power purchase arrangements.

## Reasoning
The Court differentiated contingency allocated by agreement from frustration operating independently of it. It construed the contractual exclusions and rejected the asserted force majeure relief based on increased imported coal costs. Separate change-in-law claims required their own assessment.

## Practical limits
Check the actual risk allocation, exclusions, available alternatives, notice and remedies. A change in profitability alone does not discharge a bargain. The regulatory tariff issues should not be conflated with the general contract-law analysis.
