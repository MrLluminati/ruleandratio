## Dispute resolution

The original 2006 provision establishes a conciliation stage followed by arbitration if conciliation ends unsuccessfully. It also addresses jurisdiction by reference to the supplier's location.

## Preparing a reference

Identify the relevant Council, obtain its rules and filing directions, and compile the contract, registration evidence, invoices, payment reconciliation and correspondence. Verify limitation, registration timing, procedural developments and relevant case law separately. The statutory disposal period should not be presented as a guaranteed completion date.

## Amendment and commencement review

The 2026 amendment modifies the Section 18 substitution enacted by the Mediation Act, 2023. The identified October 2026 notification does not commence amending section 8. Check later notices and the independent commencement of Mediation Act section 62 before applying the revised timelines.

The 2023 enacted version replaces conciliation with mediation. The conditional 2026 consolidation adds a ninety-day mediation period from the first appearance date, thirty days after termination for the Council to take up or refer arbitration, and ninety days from completion of pleadings to make the award. It also ties jurisdiction to the supplier's registered official address and provides for an online mechanism. Neither section 62 of the Mediation Act nor section 8 of the 2026 Amendment Act is included in the respective identified initial commencement notices. A targeted search on 27 November 2026 did not locate later commencement; this is not an exhaustive Gazette audit.

Bihar's Rules published on 29 October 2026 describe the new regime as applicable. This creates a commencement question requiring independent legal review; a State rule is not itself a Central Government commencement notification. Do not assume the validity or invalidity of the Rules or apply the new deadlines without resolving the applicable central law and savings.
