## Background

An MSEFC award was challenged under Section 34 of the Arbitration and Conciliation Act. The buyer sought waiver of the original MSMED Section 19 deposit; the lower courts refused.

## Holding

The Supreme Court held that an applicant other than the supplier cannot obtain waiver of the mandatory 75% quantum under the provision it considered. Where depositing the amount at once causes undue hardship, a court may permit instalments. The Court relied upon Goodyear India.

## Case-specific relief

An earlier interim order had allowed a lower deposit in this appeal. The Court continued that arrangement because the Section 34 proceeding was already heard and awaiting decision. Paragraph 12 expressly excluded that arrangement from precedential effect. It cannot be treated as a general exception to the mandatory quantum.

## Practical scope

Distinguish the pre-deposit from the separate requirements for setting aside or staying an award. The judgment interpreted the original Section 19. The 2026 enacted substitution requires independent commencement verification before applying its different release and territorial provisions.
