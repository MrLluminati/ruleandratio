## Statutory rate and starting point

Section 16 imposes interest at three times the RBI bank rate, compounded with monthly rests, when an eligible buyer fails to meet the Section 15 payment obligation. The rate is not the repo rate or a flat percentage fixed for all years. The statutory starting point is the appointed day where no written credit agreement applies, or the day after the agreed payment date, subject to Section 15.

## Build an invoice-wise schedule

Start with the amount actually outstanding and identify acceptance, written objections, agreed credit, due date and each payment. Obtain historical RBI bank-rate notifications covering the claimed period; record each applicable rate and its effective date. A change in rate, partial payment or disputed credit note can affect the calculation.

## Avoid misleading calculations

Do not calculate the full invoice value as outstanding after a proven payment or ignore the legal basis for allocation of remittances. Monthly rests are not the same as daily compounding. A calculator can illustrate the arithmetic but cannot determine disputed acceptance dates, supplier eligibility, payment allocation or enforceability. Keep the assumptions and calculation record available for review.
